# Decision Quality

Use for consequential corrections, Eval design, compilation, and readiness review.

## Consequence And Scope

A useful judgment changes an outcome, experience, tradeoff, scope, risk, or evaluation.
Also check whether that consequence belongs to the user's current task. High downstream
impact alone does not justify substituting a different project purpose.

Separate reported facts, user choices, AI interpretations, AI design candidates, and
unknowns. User approval of a temporary design does not establish its empirical premise.
Do not expand a short selection into an apparently user-owned rationale. An unconfirmed
implication can be proposed and discussed, but not silently enforced.

Alternatives should differ in real consequences and have credible strengths. Record
genuine rejection and reopening conditions; do not invent decoys or exclude compatible
goals merely to reduce a choice count. A useful new dimension or primary/secondary
combination may improve a judgment without eliminating a direction.

## Session Activity And Artifact Approval

`Session status` describes the ongoing conversation/work lifecycle. An `active` Session
may retain a `review-ready` or `approved` Blueprint: continuing execution within its
scope or moving into maintenance does not require pretending the Session is still at a
review gate. Keep Blueprint readiness/status about that artifact and its accepted scope.

Retain completed-stage approval as history, with its scope stated clearly. It does not
authorize new maintenance actions or a changed deliverable. Before using an old handoff,
check whether the proposed action is actually within its approved scope. If a changed
premise affects the artifact, follow the existing invalidation/recompilation rules;
otherwise preserve compatible approval. Do not relabel an active Session merely to
satisfy a validator or broaden historical approval to cover the next stage.

Pause still needs a meaningful resume point, prepared cross-task handoff still requires
pause, and current execution still requires consistent explicit approval and a non-stale
source. Structural validation cannot judge whether prose scope covers a new action.

## Eval Must Test The Actual Promise

For each consequential criterion establish:

- the current promise or claim, with its accepted source or explicit proposal status;
- the observation that could support or weaken it;
- plausible alternative explanations and the limit of the conclusion.

Use the evaluation layer the task actually needs. Technical operation can establish
usability, not automatically expressiveness or demand. Creator approval can establish
intent fit, not automatically outside-audience value. Comparative preference is necessary
when the user selected a competitive goal, not because a competitor was mentioned.
Creating a wanted work, learning from a prototype, and validating a market are legitimate
but different stage outcomes; they need not form a mandatory funnel.

For example, moving an object may test a novice onboarding goal. The same movement
alone cannot prove an experienced player's creative freedom. Conversely, experience
with another interface does not prove this interface usable. A bug, unfamiliar control,
test prompt, or novelty may explain the observation. Record insufficient evidence when
confounds prevent judging the intended claim, rather than declaring the product wrong.

The first version must be sufficient to express or test its promise, not just contain
few items. Do not invent numerical thresholds, repeated-use rules, or comparative
failure conditions. Propose them if useful, with their rationale and authority visible.
Full Taste/Intent/Eval fields are not required for every small fact or answer; preserve
the relevant connection wherever it materially constrains later work.

## Correct By Dependency, Not By Replacing The Story

On material correction:

1. Preserve the facts and accepted choices that still have support.
2. Withdraw or narrow the refuted interpretation; record the user's actual correction.
3. Identify dependent scope, examples, Eval, prohibitions, Must Ask items, and blockers.
4. Invalidate only those consequences and reopen what now needs judgment.
5. Leave the gap unknown or offer a labeled candidate; the opposite claim is not proven.

A bad justification does not permanently disqualify its design candidate. An agent's
overreach does not automatically become a durable anti-goal. Keep historical statements
in the log, and distinguish user authority from reasoning the agent supplied.

Keep provenance and historical scope in the log; Current Understanding states what now
holds with source IDs. Route/receipt point to it without retelling the whole project.
A fact can remain unknown while a temporary design is accepted; these are compatible.
A pause or compatible addition alone invalidates no approval.

## Review Current Surfaces At Meaningful Transitions

After approval, a stage transition, upstream correction, recovery or first Workbench
publication, inspect the current-facing surfaces together. Ordinary turns update only
the affected dependencies. Use this host review without adding a checklist artifact:

| Surface | Review question |
| --- | --- |
| Current Understanding and active decisions | Is this the current outcome, form, scope and authority? |
| Unknowns and module | Are resolved choices removed from pending work? Does the module describe the relevant artifact? |
| Resume point and Decision Spine | Can a fresh reader identify the actual stage and next action without restarting settled work? |
| Readiness and artifact pointers | Which artifact/version is approved, complete, pending or affected by a correction? |
| Existing Blueprint, execution and prepared handoff | Are their scope and next-action claims clearly current or historical? |
| Project state and enabled Workbench | Do overview, active record implications, details and next steps agree with the sources? |

Approval of a whole artifact settles included wording choices unless the user excludes
them. Keep earlier valid design commitments, but remove obsolete implications such as
"whole draft unapproved" from current records. History can explain what an earlier
approval did not cover; label its time/scope rather than presenting it as today's limit.
An old writing plan may stay approved after writing finishes. Mark the work complete
and identify the new stage's authority; completion alone is not a changed premise and
must not trigger blanket revocation. Conversely, new work needs its own scoped authority.

Keep unresolved semantic conflicts pending. Before `complete`, review the actual current
claims, not just metadata or hashes, and run relevant structural checks. If a source changed
while preparing edits/publication, reread and reconcile it; do not overwrite another
task's newer record. No full rewrite is needed when unaffected views remain accurate.

## Invalidate A Changed Blueprint Basis

When a Blueprint's basis changes, immediately mark Session and Blueprint reconciliation
pending and the Blueprint draft, matching header and Review State. Name the changed
basis and affected constraints in a visible stale-draft notice. Those constraints are
not operative while awaiting recompilation. Invalidate affected approval and handoffs
using the Skill's stale-execution convention; do not carry authorization into changed
scope or discard history to satisfy a validator. Old approval evidence remains historical,
while the current header and approval views reflect the changed basis.

Refresh the full Blueprint only when requested or needed at the review gate. Lazy
compilation saves work, not semantic responsibility. Before claiming the current draft
is reconciled, replace or remove stale norms across the full document, including module
fields, forbidden work, Eval failure, execution boundaries, and review brief.

## Source Entailment Review

Review every `must`, `required`, `forbidden`, `not aligned`, Eval failure, and blocker:

- What exact source authorizes this constraint at this scope?
- Does it actually follow, or is it merely compatible with that source?
- Is the source still current, and is this a commitment or an unaccepted proposal?
- Has a correction changed any dependent permission, test, or stopping condition?

For relayed input, separate the quoted or faithfully reported user choice from the
relayer's added rationale, proposed rule and requested action. A message heading such as
"user feedback" does not authorize every sentence in it. Check consequential ambiguous
authority against the available original source; otherwise keep it proposed/unknown.
Faithfully sourced existing approval does not require the user to approve it again.

Only user-authorized commitments or externally binding constraints are normative.
Facts can explain constraints without authorizing new goals. An assumption label or
valid Decision ID is not enough to make an inference a requirement. Keep useful but
unadopted choices in the proposal or assumptions portion of the draft.

Preserve people, evidence strength, scope, permissions, and quantifiers when summarizing.
A semantically changed paraphrase is a new decision. Test broad prohibitions against a
legitimate edge case: forbidding deceptive evidence synthesis does not necessarily forbid
a clearly labeled, source-traceable composite. Do not prohibit more than was chosen.

Verify references exist, but leave entailment to the host agent. Structural validation
cannot distinguish a supported requirement from an invented one with a valid ID.

## Readiness

A draft may expose unresolved choices. Review-ready requires coherent material choices,
real tradeoffs, an appropriate confirmed module, sufficient scope, claim-aligned Eval,
and explicit unknown handling. Do not manufacture a rejected direction or silently choose
a major preference just to satisfy readiness.

For a material boundary, classify allowed behavior, forbidden behavior, an important
edge case, and its resolution or evidence path. An unknown blocks only its dependent
claim or next action. Preserve uncertainty without inflating every missing historical
detail into a Blueprint blocker. Unknown result evidence need not block review of a
clearly labeled plan to obtain it; an unknown safety or permission prerequisite may.

Before reconciliation is complete, check current Session and Blueprint claims as well
as metadata. Resolved unknowns must leave unresolved lists, superseded decisions must
leave normative scope, and resume points must not restart settled work. No approval
survives a material scope change merely because an older document recorded it.

Keep the universal core across work types. Use only relevant module fields; do not force
software scope into a presentation, or market evaluation into a personal creative work.
Explain module fit and preserve user override before review-ready.
